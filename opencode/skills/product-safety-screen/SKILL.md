# Product safety screening for Sweden

Apply this workflow **before** recommending consumer products or auditing an existing recommendation list. Treat a candidate as a specific model/SKU and version, not just a brand or generic material. Respect additional exclusions or stricter thresholds from the user. Do not quietly substitute a near match for a failed candidate.

## Decision rule

Exclude a candidate when credible evidence identifies an endocrine-disrupting, carcinogenic, or otherwise toxic substance **and** realistic product use or foreseeable misuse creates meaningful human exposure to it. Also exclude a candidate with credible evidence of disrupting sleep onset, duration, or architecture, or contributing to long-term cognitive decline under realistic use. Do not equate hazard with exposure: identify substance, location in product, route, dose/duration where available, release mechanism, and strength of evidence. When a flagged substance is demonstrably contained under realistic conditions, explicitly explain why exposure is minimal. An absence of migration data is **unknown**, not proof of containment or proof of harm.

Do not recommend a product as screened/safe if decisive ingredient, construction, exposure, or availability information is unavailable. Put it in **unverified / not recommended yet**, explain what is missing, and seek a documented alternative. If no candidate passes, say so; return useful screening findings rather than filling a quota. Do not imply that absence of evidence establishes safety.

## Workflow

1. Clarify the intended product, users (including children/pregnancy if relevant), contact and use pattern, price constraints, and any non-negotiable preferences when needed. Identify foreseeable hot/cold conditions (car in summer, hot drinks, microwave, dishwasher/sterilizer, body heat), abrasion/chewing/flexing/scratching, UV/washing/hydrolysis/aging, skin area and contact time, mucosal contact/mouthing/ingestion, inhalation of sprays/dust/fumes, food transfer, and disposal/maintenance relevant to the buyer. Do not invent exposure scenarios implausible for this product.
2. Search broadly for purchasable candidates, including Swedish/EU retailers and manufacturer shops shipping to Sweden. Record actual retailer, country/shipping restriction, exact SKU, price/date if relevant, and source URL. Distinguish search-result snippets from opened pages; do not infer stock, shipping, or current price from stale snippets. Check variants separately: color, finish, batch, and model year can change materials.
3. Build a component inventory: food/body-contact surfaces, handles, linings, coatings, adhesives, dyes, inks, finishes, gaskets, batteries/electronics, and packaging where it contacts contents or contributes plausible inhalation/dermal exposure. Use manufacturer's material/BOM disclosures, SDS when applicable, independent migration/emission tests, regulatory databases, and product-specific recalls. "BPA-free," "non-toxic," certifications, and marketing copy do not settle undisclosed substitutes or other components. Ask manufacturer for composition/test reports if decisive data is missing.
4. Check **all plausible components**, not only user-named hazards, against authoritative hazard classifications and use-specific migration/emission evidence. Use blacklist below as a **search checklist**, not as an exhaustive list or automatic blanket ban. Match CAS/substance, concentration/speciation, matrix and surface, release conditions, and route. Distinguish classification (hazard) from dose/exposure; distinguish a finished article from occupational manufacturing exposure. Note regional regulation and dates.
5. Screen sleep/cognition based on product-specific mechanism and evidence (e.g., light at night, noise, stimulants, solvents, lead), realistic intensity/timing and age-specific vulnerability. Don't label all screens, fragrances, or plastics as conclusively harmful to sleep/cognition. Apply hard exclusions on credible exposure-linked evidence; otherwise explain uncertainty.
6. For products with meaningful review history, seek independent retailer/review-aggregator ratings, count, platform, date, and recurring negative reports. Prefer current model-specific reviews. If review history is sparse or inaccessible, state that instead of asserting positive reception. Reviews do not establish toxicological safety.
7. Produce compact candidate verdicts: **exclude / eligible on available evidence / unverified (not recommended yet)**. For every flagged material, state substance and component, hazard source, plausible release pathway under specific use conditions, supporting product-specific evidence or its absence, and verdict. For included candidates give Sweden purchase evidence, review rating/count/platform when meaningful, and citations. For exclusions name failed criterion; for unverified ones name next fact needed. State search date, searched sources/query scope, blind spots, and number of candidates actually checked; do not claim comprehensive market coverage from limited results.

## Blacklist / watchlist for searching

Names below flag **investigation**, not an automatic product-level finding. Exclude only according to decision rule, unless the user's additional policy explicitly imposes an absolute material ban. Check synonyms and CAS where possible. Presence of a brand, polymer family, process, or label alone is not proof of a dangerous finished product.

| Search terms / families | Check and likely pathway |
| --- | --- |
| PFAS, PFOA, PFOS, PFHxS, PFNA, fluorotelomers, FTOH, PTFE, FEP, PFA; "fluorinated," "water/stain/grease resistant," "non-stick" | Verify specific chemistry and residuals, wear/heat migration, food/dust exposure, and durability. Do not claim all fluoropolymers have identical hazard or leach profiles. |
| Bisphenols BPA, BPS, BPF and other bisphenol replacements; epoxy, polycarbonate, can linings, thermal paper | Food/skin-contact migration under heat, abrasion, aging; check substitutes rather than accepting "BPA-free." |
| Phthalates DEHP, DBP, BBP, DIBP, DINP, DIDP, DNOP; PVC/vinyl plasticizers and replacement plasticizers | Check formulation, dust, skin/food transfer, mouthing, flexing, temperature. PVC itself is a material clue, not proof a listed phthalate is present. |
| Lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury, hexavalent chromium (Cr(VI)), nickel; paints, pigments, ceramics, metal platings, leather | Test for accessible release under wear, sweat, food acids, or mouthing; evaluate nickel primarily as a sensitizer when relevant. Avoid confusing total metal content with bioavailable fraction. |
| Formaldehyde and releasers, methylene chloride, benzene, styrene, vinyl chloride, NMP, residual solvents/VOCs | Check adhesives, foam, resins, finishes, inks and emissions over real use, especially enclosed/hot spaces. Distinguish residual monomer from cured polymer. |
| Brominated/chlorinated flame retardants (PBDEs, HBCDD/HBCD, TBBPA), organophosphate flame retardants, short-chain chlorinated paraffins (SCCPs), organotins | Look for foam, electronics, textiles, plastic, coatings; evaluate contact with dust, air and degradation products; individual substances differ. |
| PAHs in rubber/recycled plastics; aromatic amines from certain azo dyes; triclosan/triclocarban; benzophenone UV filters; preservatives and fragrance ingredients | Check exact chemical, applicable hazard, leaching into skin/food/water or inhalation, dose, and sensitization if relevant. Fragrance-free and natural are not safety certifications. |
| Pesticides/biocides, antimicrobials, nano/surface treatments, silver nanoparticles; inhalable silica or fibers | Check treatment identity, particle release and route; hazard depends on form and exposure. |

Manufacturing/finish clues to investigate: fluorination or PFAS treatment; electroplating/chrome tanning; stain-resistant or flame-retardant finishes; PVC plastisol; recycled feedstock with unknown contaminant controls; solvent-based bonding, spray coatings, polyurethane foams, UV-cured resins and 3D printing; high-temperature non-stick coatings. A process is **not** itself a consumer-product failure. Ask about residuals, containment, releases in use, and quality controls. Manufacturing-worker risks belong in a separate note unless user includes ethical/occupational criteria.

Company/trade-name search aliases: **3M / Scotchgard; Chemours / DuPont / Teflon; W. L. Gore / Gore-Tex**. Use these to find specific treatment chemistry, historical formulations, and batch-specific product disclosures; never blacklist an entire company or every product carrying a trademark by association. Search other brands, supplier names, recalls and aliases revealed by the candidate's components. Product-specific recall or enforcement action warrants a check of model, region, batch, date, and remedy, not guilt by shared manufacturer name.

## Evidence and browsing discipline

Use actual accessible sources and direct links for product and hazard claims. Start with primary resources as appropriate: [ECHA chemical information](https://echa.europa.eu/information-on-chemicals), [ECHA Candidate List](https://echa.europa.eu/candidate-list-table), [IARC Monographs](https://monographs.iarc.who.int/), [US EPA CompTox](https://comptox.epa.gov/dashboard), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), [EU Safety Gate](https://ec.europa.eu/safety-gate-alerts/screen/webReport), and [Swedish Chemicals Agency](https://www.kemi.se/en). These links are **starting points**, not citations for a specific product verdict; cite the exact classification/study/report and manufacturer listing actually read, with dates where possible. Note disagreement and study limitations (animal vs. human, hazard vs. exposure, tested vs. untested variant). Do not fabricate links, quotes, regulatory determinations, ratings, inventory or negative search findings.

If live web search, interactive browser, retailer checkout, or a source page is unavailable, disclose which capability failed. Try available web-fetch/search tools, directly known source URLs, local documents, or user-provided links; distinguish each from live market search. State exactly which sites/queries were attempted and which could be opened. If no usable external retrieval exists, do **not** provide current purchasable-in-Sweden or review claims; ask for retailer/manufacturer links or material disclosures, or give a clearly labeled preliminary screening checklist. Never imply all products on the Swedish market were surveyed.

## Sub-agent orchestration (when available)

For multi-candidate or multi-component searches, delegate **independent read-only research** concurrently when a sub-agent tool is available and permitted. Ask one agent for Sweden availability/SKU/reviews; another for composition and use-condition migration/emission; another for hazard classifications, sleep/cognition evidence and model-specific recalls. For large candidate sets, shard by SKU with same evidence checklist; avoid duplicating searches. Give each agent precise scope, source URLs to verify, no-edit instruction, and required output: SKU, URL/date, substance/component, hazard, exposure route, conclusion, unknowns. Integrate results yourself, resolve conflicting claims by checking original sources, and apply final eligibility rule centrally. If no sub-agent tool is available, run same tracks sequentially; never describe research as delegated when it was not. Do not delegate simply to make one trivial lookup look comprehensive.

## One-shot examples (illustrative outcomes, not market facts)

**Prompt:** "Recommend a reusable bottle for hot tea in Sweden; I dishwasher-clean it daily." **Response pattern:** "Model A (illustrative): unverified - listing says stainless steel, but lid gasket/coating materials and hot-liquid migration data are missing; ask for lid composition and test report. Model B (illustrative): exclude if product-specific evidence confirms BPA migration from hot-contact lining at meaningful exposure. I checked [actual listings/tests], on [date]; no verified recommendation from these two candidates." Never infer safe lid from steel body.

**Prompt:** "Is a PTFE-coated pan automatically disqualified?" **Response pattern:** "No automatic verdict from PTFE name alone. Identify coating construction and residuals, normal high-temperature use, foreseeable overheating, scratches, food contact and emissions; cite relevant product-specific measurements and hazard classifications. If exposure-linked hard exclusion is supported, exclude; if data are missing, mark unverified. Clearly separate normal-use data from overheating data."

**Prompt:** "Find a Swedish baby teether without harmful plasticizers; shopping sites aren't opening." **Response pattern:** "Current Sweden stock/reviews unverified: [named sites or tools] could not be opened on [date]. Candidate X's manufacturer [actual URL if accessible] discloses [material]; plasticizer identity and mouthing migration are missing, so not recommended yet. Provide a manufacturer/retailer link or test report for screening. No claim of complete Swedish market coverage."
